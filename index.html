A patient-owned record for PMOS

A cancelled visit, filled by someone who's ready.

PMOS is the new name for PCOS. Attune is a record she owns and shares with her PCP, gyno and endo, so she stops retelling her story at every visit. When a specialist has a cancellation, Attune offers the time to a patient who is already referred by her PCP, complete for that practice's criteria, insurance-checked, and has her record on one page. People approve every step.

Discovery

Where referrals break today

From 49 discovery interviews with patients, PCP staff, specialist intake staff and OB/GYNs. The patient is the only one who sees the whole handoff.

  • PCP office

    The medical assistant pulls notes and labs from separate systems and faxes them by hand. Finding an in-network specialist with a working fax number takes the longest. After the fax, nobody knows what happened.

  • Specialist office

    Referrals arrive as faxes in the EHR inbox with partial records. Schedulers call the patient, and the provider retakes the history even when notes were sent.

  • Patient

    She bounces between providers, chases the referral herself and retells her history from memory. Some skip the referral entirely because of insurance or the wait.

How the fill works

Eight steps, one record

Steps 1 to 4 build and check the referral. Step 6 fills the cancellation. Step 8 returns the outcome to her record.

  1. She starts her record.Three questions, then a one-minute check-in on mood, sleep, energy and skin.
  2. She shares it with her PCP.A share code or QR plus her date of birth. The MA sees what she shared on one page.
  3. The PCP office refers.Gyno or endo, a written reason, and a physician signs "I evaluated this patient."
  4. Attune checks it.Complete against each practice's published criteria, then insurance from a photo of her card.
  5. She joins the waitlist.She sets distance, telehealth yes or no, days and times, and the shortest notice she can handle.
  6. A practice posts a cancellation.Attune offers it to ready patients with a countdown. The first to accept gets it; the others hear "That slot just filled. You're still on the list."
  7. The front desk opens her Patient Pack.Her story in her own words, the referral note, labs, check-in trends, meds and insurance. She confirms nothing has changed the day before.
  8. The summary comes back.The specialist uploads a visit summary. It lands in her record and her PCP's inbox, and the referral closes.

Who gets an offer

  • Her referral is PCP-signed and complete for that practice's criteria.
  • Insurance was checked in the last 24 hours, and the practice accepts her plan.
  • Same specialty, within her distance, or telehealth if she said yes.
  • Inside her days and times, and no sooner than her shortest notice.
  • No other offer is already waiting for her.

In this order

  1. The PCP's urgency flag
  2. Longest on the waitlist
  3. Nearest

Never by who pays, by payer or by plan type. Practices never see the queue or pick patients. Their only lever is the criteria they publish.

For patients

Her record, shared on her terms. A minute a day, then it ends.

  • Three questions, then a one-minute check-in. Home ends with "You're caught up." No red dots, no badges, no endless feed.
  • Share by section, with each office. Turn any section off for any office at any time, and see who viewed her record and when.
  • Offers instead of phone tag. When a time opens that fits her preferences, she gets it with a countdown. Accepting is her consent to share her Patient Pack with that office.
  • No health details in texts. A notification only says a time opened. Everything else stays in the app.

For specialist offices

Post a cancellation. A patient who's ready takes it.

  • Post an opening. Provider, time, in person or telehealth. Before you post, you see how long the hold runs and how many patients get it.
  • Ready patients only. Everyone offered is PCP-signed, complete for the criteria you publish, and insurance-checked. You can't see the queue or pick patients.
  • The Patient Pack. Once she accepts: her story, the referral note, labs, check-in trends, meds and insurance on one page, each with its source. Print it to PDF. Display only.
  • Close the loop. Mark the visit attended and send a summary to her record and her PCP. Unfilled openings say why, as counts only.

Rules

Rules Attune follows

These are built into the shared record's rules and covered by tests, not only written down.

  • She approves every share.Consent is per section and per office, can be withdrawn, and is logged. She sees who viewed her record. An office may keep what it already received.
  • Accepting is consent.A practice sees nothing about her until she accepts its opening. Then it gets her Patient Pack.
  • A physician signs every referral.The PCP decides who qualifies for a referral. Attune never signs.
  • Never ranked by who pays.Offers follow the PCP's urgency flag, time on the waitlist and distance. Fees, payer and plan type never enter the order.
  • Display only, never a diagnosis.Attune doesn't name conditions, score severity or recommend treatment. Every item shows where it came from.
  • No health details in texts.Notifications say only that a time opened, and fit in one text message.

Where Attune is today

Attune is a prototype with synthetic data, not for real patients. Matching, offers, hold timers and statuses run on real rules. Insurance checks, the provider directory and EMR connections are mocked, and nothing is sent to a real office.

The prototype shows a fee ledger for specialist practices and charges nothing. No fee will be billed before a healthcare attorney reviews it. PCPs and patients pay nothing for the referral loop. Attune does not diagnose or treat any condition.
