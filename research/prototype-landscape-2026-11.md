# Prototype landscape: waitlist fill, B2B2C, PMOS apps, consent, design

*2026-11-18 · Attune. Claims I could not confirm are marked **(unverified)**.*

Companion to [competitive-landscape.md](competitive-landscape.md). Products covered there come up here only when there is a new, sourced fact.

## At a glance

Real waitlist products send each opening to several patients at once, and the first to accept gets it. At Sutter, Epic Fast Pass offered each slot to 5 patients at once. Windows ran from 30 minutes for same-day slots to overnight, and other systems use 2, 3 or 12 hours. Per-offer acceptance is 8–11% in peer-reviewed data. More than half of Sutter's offers were opened only after the slot had expired or been taken, so the "slot is gone" screen is the one most patients will see. Attune's single-offer tiers are slower than market practice and only work if preference matching lifts acceptance well above 10%. In B2B2C health, payers, employers or practices pay. Apart from Zocdoc, I found no model where the receiving clinician pays per referral. Existing PMOS apps are symptom journals with "not a diagnosis" disclaimers. Of these, only Hertility (UK) and Midi route patients to a clinical next step, and Midi added a PMOS pathway in August 2026. On consent, Apple, MyChart and SMART Health Links offer patterns worth copying: per-topic toggles, access logs, expiring codes, and a plain statement that data already sent cannot be recalled.

## What the prototype should copy, and what it should avoid

1. **Offer text carries no health detail.** Keep it to 160 characters or fewer. Leave out the specialty, practice, clinician and condition, and put them behind sign-in. Example, 137 characters with a 27-character link: `Attune: an earlier appointment opened, Tue Dec 3, 9:40 AM. Held for you until 10:25 AM. <link> Reply STOP to opt out`. The lock-screen push says only "Attune · An earlier time opened." (§1 Fast Pass flyers, NexHealth, TCPA, HHS)
2. **Say when it's a race.** In the 2- and 3-offer tiers, don't say "held." Use: `Attune: an earlier appointment opened, Tue Dec 3, 9:40 AM. Sent to 3 people; first to accept gets it. <link> Reply STOP to opt out` (151 characters). (§1 JHAH)
3. **Design the "taken" and "expired" states first.** At Sutter, 53% of offers were opened after the slot had expired or gone. Taken: "Someone else accepted this time first. You're still on the list, and your place hasn't changed," plus any other open times. Expired: "This hold ended at 10:25 AM. Nothing was shared." Never count either against her. (§1 Sutter, NexHealth, UCSF)
4. **Hold windows: keep the table, but add quiet hours and a fallback.** Start no new holds between 9 PM and 7 AM local time. An evening offer for a slot more than 24h out holds until 9 AM, as Sutter's evening batches ran until 5:30 AM and evening offers were accepted more often. If a slot more than 48h out is still unfilled after 4 single offers, switch to 2 at a time. In the under-4h tier, offer telehealth- and short-notice-preferring patients first. Log per-offer acceptance: below about 15%, the single-offer tiers take close to a day (computed). (§1 tier table, Sutter, UCSF)
5. **Cap offers and watch fairness.** Send at most 1 offer text per day and 3 per week per patient, because repeat offers are accepted less often. After 3 declines, ask her to update her preferences instead of quietly dropping her. Send offers in her language, and report acceptance by age band and language. (§1 TCPA, Sutter, UCSF)
6. **Exclude and release.** Don't offer a slot to a patient who already has an equal or earlier qualifying appointment. When she accepts, Attune releases her old booking and tells that office, rather than leaving it to staff. (§1 NexHealth)
7. **The specialist sees the slot, not the patient, until she accepts.** The practice view shows offered, held until 10:25, accepted or expired. The Patient Pack and its audit event appear only after acceptance. This differs from Fast Pass and NexHealth, which offer slots only to the practice's own patients. (§1 NexHealth, Fast Pass)
8. **Consent comes before the accept button.** Name the recipient practice, list the Patient Pack sections with a toggle for each, and add one line: "Once sent, this office keeps what it received. Stopping sharing stops future updates only." (§4 Apple, §3 Josie)
9. **"Who has seen my record" log.** Each entry shows the recipient, date and time, and sections viewed, using MyChart's columns. Revoke sits on the same row. (§4 MyChart)
10. **The specialist link is a SMART Health Link with guardrails.** Require a passcode or DOB check, lock out after 3 wrong tries, and keep the label free of diagnosis and under 80 characters. Revoking it in Attune makes it return 404, and every open is logged. (§4 Share Everywhere, SHL spec, Flexpa)
11. **Distribution and money.** The MA texts the patient a web link during the visit: no install, no password at first touch. B2B2C analogs are paid by payers, employers or practices, so keep the per-referral fee ledger display-only. (§2 Klara/ModMed, MGMA, Pomelo, Twentyeight, Maven)
12. **Copy and look.** Use symptom-journal language: never "late," "abnormal," "diagnosed" or "you have." Put a "Not a diagnosis" line on the Patient Pack. Use a serif display face with a plain sans on a warm cream base, check contrast, and end the home screen with "You're caught up." (§3 Josie, Pomaia, Flo, Natural Cycles, Hertility; §5 Tia, Cora, Midi, Instagram)
